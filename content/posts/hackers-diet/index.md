I was chubby all my life, and long ago I accepted it as part of my identity. Even when I exercised and spent most of my free time outside chasing a ball, I could not lose weight. Throughout my school years I was overweight, and nice clothes never came in my size.

But this is not a story about how hard that was. You can imagine it: bullies, xenophobia, finger-pointing and little support at home, where my father wanted a slim, football-playing son I could never be. No, this is a story about how I got out of obesity: the pitfalls, how I finally lost 50kg, and how I managed to **stay** at that weight for years.

It may help you if you want to lose weight, if someone close to you struggles with their weight, or if you are simply curious.

My journey has convinced me that maintaining a stable weight concerns all of us. Obesity and type 2 diabetes are now so common that your doctor will probably tell you to lose weight sooner or later. The alternative is dying of cardiovascular disease, most likely a heart attack. (If not, you are one of the few genetically lucky ones.)

That is what happened to me years ago when I visited my general practitioner. I was an obese, single programmer who spent too much time at work, sitting in a chair and living on snacks, energy drinks and liters of milky coffee. I was a workaholic. I spent nearly the whole day coding or browsing the internet for ways to do my job better. I was a true greenhorn, chasing the best design patterns and silver bullets, and I loved being praised by my senior colleagues. I was perfect material for burnout, and sure enough, I burned out.

The revelation came one long night while I was working on a side project at home. In those days I worked all day at the office, walked home in the evening and then spent the whole night on a hobby project. It was after midnight and I was eating my second dinner. Non-stop thinking takes calories, and I was binging a lot...

One such evening, sitting at the table, I looked down at my belly. All I could see was an obese body, and I began to wonder how I had ended up like this. I had always been chubby, but never obese. At that moment I somehow decided that my weight was a real problem.

So I started with the gold standard: eat less and move more. I chose an hour of swimming every second day, because I was too heavy for anything else. Determination was never my problem. Fighting bullies at school had made me resilient and strong-minded, and once I accept that a problem is real, I always give it my best. With such regular exercise, one morning my trousers simply fell off, and I saw the first visible change.

Once I had lost a few kilos, I could finally return to other sports I used to do, starting with running. I never liked sprints, but I enjoy endurance sports. I started slowly with 3km and 4km and within a few months I was regularly running 8km, while continuing to swim. It was a very powerful combination, and the results became very, VERY visible.

I ended up in the best physical shape of my life. Regular sport changed my motivation, so I worked fewer night hacking shifts and my sleep improved a lot. I had plenty of energy and lost my craving for sugar, energy drinks and endless coffee. Motivated, I started joining organized runs. I even had a new problem with clothes, going from a tight XXL to a very comfortable L. I felt that I was back ... finally.

If the story ended here, it would be only half of the truth, and it would read like clickbait. Life is more complicated than news headlines, as I learned the hard way ...

> Losing weight is easy. Keeping it off is the true art.

My weight roller coaster began simply: I fell in love ❤...

There is nothing unusual about falling in love, except that your priorities change. That is exactly what happened to me. My spartan life ended, and more urgent matters took over.

There was always something more important than going for a run. I wanted to spend as much time as I could with my wife, watching films, having dinner together, visiting family and going to feasts. We moved to a new place and started living together, and in time we had two wonderful kids.

My regular sporting habits fell apart, though I did not stop completely. I still swam at the local pool and started running in the nearby hills, but rarely compared to the nearly daily training before, and I was getting chubbier again.

I thought then, and still think now, that it was a good compromise. Newborn babies take two adults to handle, and I needed to ride this tandem with my wife. That is what dads do.

The nail in the coffin was COVID-19 and the lockdown. I spent many months working from home, and my weight climbed again until it was back over 130kg. With everything closed, swimming was no longer an option, and I was too heavy to run. I tried doing yoga with my wife, but I was so obese that I could not keep it up. Yoga is hard when you are obese.

Eventually I had to take my old oversized clothes out of a box. I packed away my lean shirts with a groan, hoping to wear them again one day. I was depressed and felt like a complete failure... I knew I had to lose weight and change something, but like many obese people, I had no idea where to start. I had the determination to change, as I have been a fighter since childhood, yet I felt powerless.

Then something totally unexpected happened ... I got lucky.

The YouTube algorithm showed me a lecture by an American doctor, Dr. Pradip Jamnadas, about intermittent fasting and the ketogenic diet. In a nutshell, he advised eating once a day on a low-carb diet designed to trigger ketone bodies. Changing what you eat should be enough to bring your weight down.

Surely he was a quack, I thought. Still, he said you could easily measure ketones in your morning urine with a strip from the pharmacy, so I gave it a try. I bought some strips and ate once a day (a confit duck for starters :)). The next morning I tested my urine and measured a decent amount of ketones.

It worked! That small success amazed me and gave me the kick I needed to keep eating once a day for months. I had no idea how deep this rabbit hole went or where it would lead. I kept fasting for the next 7 months and lost 50kg. I did no heavy workouts and never went to the gym, which was closed because of the pandemic anyway. I mostly sat in a chair in my home office and worked in the garden, while the weight kept coming off. It suited what I could do during COVID, and it worked perfectly.

Is there more to this story? Actually ... there is.

I was so amazed that this was possible that I spent all my time during COVID going down this rabbit hole. My wife joined the research along the way. She is an MD, and she was surprised as well, since nutrition was not part of the medical curriculum when she was at university. We read scientific papers and learned a ton. Much of this research is recent, and there is far more science in fasting than just being hungry.

For one thing, your body secretes less ghrelin and neuropeptide Y, which means less hunger, fewer cravings and fewer calories consumed. For another, a ketogenic diet lowers insulin to levels where your body starts to prefer burning fat over everything else. Without large insulin spikes, food cravings fade as well. Very convenient when you want to lose 50kg!

The standard Western diet, on the other hand, is full of carbs, which are added nearly everywhere. If you do not read every package label, you may be surprised by how much sugar you consume, and by how many calories there are in chips and nuts. You can burn them off if you train often, but expect the yo-yo effect when you stop.

That is what happened to me years earlier. I was unprepared and did not understand many details of nutrition.

What are calories? Is there a difference in how we metabolize sugars, fats and proteins? Can food program your genes to boost lipogenesis in the liver? What exactly happens in your body after you eat? How do you build a schedule that lets you maintain your weight in the long term? How do you prepare your mind to resist the obesogenic environment we live in? And how do you lose weight in a way you can sustain for years?

The answer goes deeper than missing knowledge of biochemistry. It is also a social problem. Food is very hard to resist, food advertising is everywhere, and we are used to calming our stress by eating. All of this can lead to a vicious circle: "in stress -> binging -> be in stress from binging -> more binging".

My wife and I realized that this knowledge about managing weight was worth sharing. Good things must spread... So we started a YouTube channel, [Metabolicky ridicak](https://www.youtube.com/@metabolickyridicak) (only in Czech), about what we have learned about dieting.

We see many people around us suffering from diabetes, obesity and high cholesterol, with no good way to start. What can they cook? What do they need for effective fasting? The channel offers short videos with quick recipes, deep dives into metabolism, reviews of diets we have tested and our own pitfalls. All of this can help convince viewers that "problem is a problem that should be targeted effectively".

> All materials/videos are open and free, as open source / open science. As it should be ...

We are already seeing results. My pre-diabetic mom has lost 14 kg. Even though she is conservative about food, she was able to change her diet gradually and find one that works for her, rather than working for a diet. Now she even finds time for regular exercise!

My journey has convinced me that even our biggest weakness can become an advantage. Yes, it can take time, with pitfalls and looping in circles along the way, but that is normal. We are only human. There is no silver bullet diet, and all "good advice" has to be adapted to a person's context, preferences, lifestyle and limits. Only then does it work, when a short-term diet turns into a long-term habit.

All the best to anyone starting their own weight loss journey ...

My journey continues ... ⛵
